## Question
How many travellers are added vs removed per group; is there an add/remove churn?

## Answer
Across all 1,200 groups (`group_started`, sample 2026-06-08 → 2026-06-28), `traveller_added` logged **3,495 add rows** (avg **2.91 adds/group**) against `group_started.group_size` ranging 2–6. `traveller_removed` logged only **70 removal rows**, touching **69 distinct groups** (68 groups with exactly 1 removal, 1 group with 2) — every removed `group_id` is confirmed present in both `group_started` and `traveller_added` (69/69, 100%), so removals are consistent, not orphaned events.

Net effect: **3,425 travellers net-added** (3,495 − 70). Churn is real but small: **5.75% of groups (69/1,200)** had at least one removal, and only **2.00% of add-events (70/3,495)** were later offset by a removal. So the dominant group-flow pattern is pure addition — the vast majority (94.25%) of groups never remove a co-traveller once added; where churn does occur it's almost always a single removal, not repeated churn (only 1 group removed twice).

**Scope/caveats:** sample is 2026-06-08→2026-06-28 only (per `load_report.md`); per known_issues.md D2, this spec's `application_id` has 0% overlap with `application_started`, so this is analysed standalone (not joined to the main funnel); per D6/table docs, `traveller_added`/`traveller_removed` legitimately break the "one row per user" pattern (a group owner can add/remove multiple co-travellers), which is accounted for here by aggregating on `group_id` rather than assuming 1 row = 1 user. `traveller_removed`'s n=70 is small — treat any further segment cut (by relation, device, geo) as directional only.
