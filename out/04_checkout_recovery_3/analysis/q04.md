## Question
Segment cuts (device, geo, destination). Bonus: does recovery target the same drop-offs seen in the existing funnel tables?

## Answer

Live query against `clickathon.abandonment_detected` (2,300 rows, sample window
2026-06-08→2026-07-01) joined by `user_id` set-membership (not `windowFunnel`,
per known_issues.md D1) against `clickathon.reconverted` (93 rows) gives the
recovery rate — `reconverted ÷ abandonment_detected` — per segment. First,
re-checked the row counts directly: `count() = uniqExact(user_id) = 2,300` on
`abandonment_detected` and `93/93` on `reconverted`, with no growth vs. the
documented figures — **this settles known_issues.md → D2's open
"was spec 04 double-loaded?" question: no, the live table holds exactly one
copy of the data**, not 4,600/186.

**By device (`device_type`):** flat, 3.1%–4.8%, no real segment story — iOS
971 dropped/42 recovered (4.33%), Android 745/30 (4.03%), Desktop 168/8
(4.76%), web-user-b2c 416/13 (3.12%, the one laggard).

**By geo (`geoip_country_code`):** volume is India-dominated (1,390 of 2,300
drops, 60%, at 4.53% recovery) with everything else in the low hundreds.
Small-n but a real spread: AU best at 4.9% (n=102), SG 4.5% (n=222), IN 4.53%
(n=1,390), AE 3.35% (n=239), GB 2.65% (n=113), US 1.95% (n=154), SA worst at
1.25% (n=80, i.e. 1 recovery out of 80).

**By destination:** 14 destinations appear in this sample (not all 27),
138–191 drops each — too thin to trust individually, but TR (5.95%, n=168)
and EG (5.23%, n=172) lead, JP is worst (2.21%, n=181). No destination
clears ~6%; this cut is noisy at this sample size and shouldn't drive a
channel/targeting decision on its own.

**Bonus — does recovery targeting match the real funnel's drop-offs? No,
and the mismatch is large.** `abandonment_detected.drop_step` records which
of the 4 real funnel tables the user last touched before being flagged:

| `drop_step` (= last real-funnel stage touched) | Flagged for recovery | True non-progressors at that transition (live counts: 1,000,000 / 154,413 / 20,446 / 14,739) | Flag rate | Recovery rate of flagged |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `destination_card_clicked` (never started an application) | 686 | 845,587 | **0.081%** | 2.62% |
| `application_started` (never uploaded a document) | 521 | 133,967 | **0.39%** | 4.80% |
| `document_uploaded` (never clicked pay-now) | 696 | 5,707 | **12.20%** | 4.45% |
| `pay_now_clicked` (never purchased) | 397 | 7,685 | **5.17%** | 4.79% |

The real funnel's two biggest leaks by far are card-click→application-start
(845,587 lost, 84.56% drop) and application-start→document-upload (133,967
lost, 86.76% drop — "the biggest leak" per tables/index.md) — together
**98.6% of all non-converters in the whole funnel**. Yet recovery flags only
a token fraction of each (0.08% and 0.39% respectively). The system instead
concentrates recovery effort, proportionally, on the two *smaller* late-stage
leaks: it flags document_uploaded-stage droppers at **12.2%** and
pay_now_clicked-stage droppers at **5.2%** — 30–150× the flag rate applied to
the much larger early-funnel leaks. Recovery is **not** targeting the
funnel's actual worst drop-offs; it is weighted toward the smallest ones.
(Recovery rate *of what does get flagged* is fairly flat, 2.6%–4.8%, across
all four steps, so this isn't a "recovery works better there" story — it's a
targeting/detection-coverage story.)

**Caveats:** per known_issues.md D2, this spec's `application_id` has 0%
overlap with `application_started` and its `user_id` has never been
overlap-checked against the main funnel either — so this comparison trusts
`drop_step`'s label semantics and compares independent population *counts*,
not a verified cross-table join of the same users. Segment sample sizes
below device level (geo, destination) are in the low hundreds and should be
read directionally, not as precise rates. All step-through/recovery figures
here are the spec's first-ever verified query (no prior `analysis/qNN.md`
existed for spec 04) — computed by `user_id` set-membership per D1, not
`windowFunnel` or timestamp ordering.
