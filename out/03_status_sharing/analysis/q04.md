## Question
Which destinations spread most?

## Answer
By raw reach, **AU (Australia)** spreads the most: 223 link opens (of 2,310
total, 14 destinations observed) — the most of any destination — across 86
distinct shares opened and 135 opens by new-to-platform recipients, both
also the highest of any destination. VN (187 opens/73 shares) and TH (175
opens/71 shares) follow. By viral *efficiency* instead of volume — the rate
at which an opened link converts into a recipient CTA click
(`recipient_cta_clicked` ÷ `link_opened`, the spec's own K-factor
numerator/denominator pair) — **AE (UAE)** leads at 16.37% (28/171), ahead
of US (15.32%, 19/124) and FR (15.29%, 26/170); AU itself is mid-pack on
this cut at 10.76% (24/223). So "spreads most" has two different correct
answers depending on whether the PM means raw reach (AU) or conversion
efficiency (AE) — both are reported below since the question doesn't
specify which.

| Destination | Opens | Distinct shares opened | New-user opens | CTA clicks | CTA rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| AU | 223 | 86 | 135 | 24 | 10.76% |
| VN | 187 | 73 | 107 | 23 | 12.30% |

**Scope and method.** Query: `link_opened` and `recipient_cta_clicked`,
`clickathon` DB, grouped directly by each table's own `destination` column
(`FixedString(2)`, ISO-2 uppercase, 14 values observed) — sample window
2026-06-08 06:00 → 2026-07-01 09:21 (per `profile.md`; the only window
available for spec 03). 2,310 `link_opened` rows and 305
`recipient_cta_clicked` rows total. Deliberately did **not** join
`link_opened` ↔ `recipient_cta_clicked` on `share_id` — per
[known_issues.md](../../../context/known_issues.md) D1 and
[relationship.md](../../../context/relationship.md), that sharer-side ↔
recipient-side `share_id` join has **not yet been set-membership verified**
for this spec (no prior `analysis/qNN.md` existed before this run). Both
tables carry their own `destination` attribute directly, so this answer
sidesteps that unverified join entirely and reports each table's
independent per-destination aggregate rather than a joined per-share rate.
`link_opened` itself has fan-out (2,310 opens over only 922 distinct
`share_id`s, i.e. links get reopened), so "opens" and "distinct shares
opened" are reported separately — they agree on ranking here (AU tops
both) but need not in general. 14-destination sample is fairly evenly
spread (opens range 124–223, ~1.8×), so no destination is a dramatic
outlier either way.
