## Question
Recipient → new-application conversion (a K-factor): opens → `recipient_cta_clicked` among `recipient_is_new_user`.

## Answer
First, the join this metric depends on: `recipient_cta_clicked.share_id ⊆ link_opened.share_id` is **100% verified** by live set-membership (all 263 distinct `share_id`s behind a CTA click are present among `link_opened`'s 922 distinct `share_id`s) — this closes an open D1 item for spec 03 (the wiki previously flagged this join as not-yet-verified).

Because a link can be reopened (`link_opened` has 2,310 rows over only 922 distinct `share_id`s) and `recipient_cta_clicked` is a **share-level** event (not tied to a specific open), the metric is measured at the `share_id` grain: *does this opened share ever produce a CTA click ("start_own_application")?*

**Data-quality finding surfaced while computing this:** `recipient_is_new_user` is not stable per `share_id` — when a link is reopened, different opens of the *same* share sometimes carry `true` and sometimes `false`. **472 of 922 shares (51.2%) show both values across their opens.** This is the same shape of self-contradiction as the known `is_crossed_failed_attempt_threshold` flag (D3) and materially affects how this K-factor should be read.

Splitting shares into those where the flag is **internally consistent across every open** ("pure") isolates the real signal:

| Segment | Distinct shares | Converted to CTA click | Conversion |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **Pure new-user** (`recipient_is_new_user=true` on every open) | 299 | 114 | **38.13%** |
| **Pure existing-user** (`false` on every open) | 151 | **0** | **0.00%** |
| Mixed / conflicting flag | 472 | 149 | 31.57% |

Not a single one of the 151 unambiguously-existing-user shares produced a `recipient_cta_clicked` — sensible, since the CTA is literally "start your own application," which an existing user doesn't need. If instead you count any share that was *ever* flagged new-user at least once (the looser, "any" definition — 771 shares, including the 472 mixed ones), the rate is 34.11% (263/771) vs. 23.92% (149/623) for any-existing-user shares; the mixed shares mechanically pull both numbers toward each other, which is why the pure split above is the more trustworthy read.

**Headline:** the recipient → new-application K-factor is real and large for genuinely new-user recipients (**~34–38%** of opened shares go on to click "start your own application"), and effectively **zero** for recipients the platform already recognizes as existing users, once the self-contradicting `recipient_is_new_user` flag is filtered down to its unambiguous subset. Scope: Status Sharing sample, 2026-06-08 06:00 → 2026-07-01 09:21, 922 distinct opened shares / 263 distinct shares with a CTA click; this flow is standalone (does not join `application_started` — 0% `application_id` overlap, D2) and the flag-conflict rate (51.2%) should be reported alongside any single conversion number from this table.
