## Question
Reconversion (recovery) rate by **drop_step** — which step is most recoverable?

## Answer
`application_started` is the most recoverable drop step at **4.80%** (25/521), essentially tied with `pay_now_clicked` at **4.79%** (19/397). `document_uploaded` trails a bit at **4.45%** (31/696), and `destination_card_clicked` is clearly the worst-recovering step at **2.62%** (18/686) — roughly half the rate of the other three. Overall recovery rate across all steps is 93/2,300 = 4.04%, matching the wiki's headline figure.

| drop_step | abandoned (n) | reconverted (n) | recovery rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| application_started | 521 | 25 | **4.80%** |
| pay_now_clicked | 397 | 19 | 4.79% |
| document_uploaded | 696 | 31 | 4.45% |
| destination_card_clicked | 686 | 18 | 2.62% |

Query: joined `clickathon.abandonment_detected` (2,300 rows, 1/user) to `clickathon.reconverted` (93 rows, 1/user) on `user_id`, grouped by `abandonment_detected.drop_step`. `user_id` was used deliberately instead of `application_id`, because `application_id` is confirmed 0% overlap against `application_started` for all 6 Abandoned Checkout Recovery tables (known_issues.md D2) — this spec must be analyzed standalone via its own `user_id`/`drop_step` keys.

This also **upgrades the wiki's caveat** on this figure: the tables/index and `abandonment_detected.md`/`reconverted.md` pages flag the recovery step-through as an "unverified row-count ratio" pending a set-membership check (D1) — that check has now run live. Result: **100% of `reconverted`'s 93 `user_id`s are a verified subset of `abandonment_detected`'s 2,300** (no orphans), and **100% of matched rows carry the same `drop_step`** in both tables (93/93) — so this breakdown is a genuine verified join, not a coincidental row-count ratio. Sample scope: Abandoned Checkout Recovery data, 2026-06-08 06:01 → 2026-07-01 00:00; n is small per step (397–696 abandoned, 18–31 recovered), so the gap between the top three steps (4.45–4.80%) should be read as noisy/near-tied, while `destination_card_clicked`'s clearly lower 2.62% is the more robust signal — users who drop at the very first step (before even starting an application) recover at roughly half the rate of those who dropped further into the funnel.
