## Question
Which segments adopt Express most (device, geo, saved-method type)?

## Answer
"Adoption" is measured as the **Express selection rate**: `express_checkout_selected` users ÷ `express_checkout_shown` users who saw the button, within each segment (verified as a valid set-membership join — 100% of the 1,007 `selected` users are a subset of the 1,650 `shown` users, so the semi-join is exact, not a row-count approximation). Sample: 2026-06-08 → 2026-06-28 (the only window loaded for this spec), 1,650 shown / 1,007 selected overall (61.03%).

**Device** (n=shown): `android` leads at **62.83%** (338/538), then `ios` 60.97% (428/702), `Desktop` 60.87% (56/92), `web-user-b2c` 58.18% (185/318). The spread is only ~4.7pp — device is not a strong differentiator.

**Geo** (`geoip_country_code`, n=shown): `AU` highest at **67.9%** (55/81), then `SA` 64.62% (42/65), `SG` 63.95% (94/147), `US` 62.3% (76/122), `GB` 61.33% (46/75), `IN` 60.18% (606/1,007 — by far the largest population, 61% of all shown), `AE` lowest at 57.52% (88/153). Range is ~10pp, but every non-IN country has n=65–153, so AU/SA/SG's lead is directional, not statistically solid at this sample size; IN is the only geo with a large enough base to trust its rate closely.

**Saved-method type** (`saved_method_type` on `express_checkout_selected` — there's no "shown" denominator for method type, so this is a share of the 1,007 selections, not a rate): `card` 33.96% (342), `upi` 33.47% (337), `wallet` 32.57% (328) — essentially an even three-way split (≤1.4pp spread), no method dominates adoption.

**Bottom line:** geo shows the clearest (if still modest) skew — Australia/Saudi Arabia/Singapore adopt Express ~5–8pp more than the UAE — while device type and saved-method type show little to no meaningful segmentation. Caveats: this is a standalone-flow analysis (per known_issues.md D2, all 5 Express Checkout tables' `application_id` returned 0% overlap with `application_started`, so this cannot be joined to the main funnel or to user demographics beyond what's in these tables); the 3-week sample is small, especially per-country; and `device_type` casing (`Desktop` vs `android`/`ios`/`web-user-b2c`) here is a genuine 4-category domain for this table, not a D9 casing collision needing normalization.
