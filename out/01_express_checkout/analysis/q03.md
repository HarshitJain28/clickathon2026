## Question
How much faster is Express (`payment.latency_ms`, time from shown → confirmed)?

## Answer
This can't be answered as a "faster than standard checkout" comparison — only the Express side of the number exists in usable form.

**What Express looks like on its own:** `express_payment_confirmed.payment_latency_ms` (flattened from `payment.latency_ms`) is populated on all 836 rows: mean **2,305.5 ms**, median **2,341.5 ms**, range 607–3,999 ms, stddev ≈958 ms. Sample window 2026-06-08 → 2026-06-28.

**Why a "standard checkout" baseline can't be computed:** there is no `latency_ms` field anywhere on the standard flow (`pay_now_clicked`, `purchase_completed`) — the only way to approximate it is `purchase_completed.timestamp − pay_now_clicked.timestamp`, joined on `user_id` (7,054 matched pairs). Two things break that:
1. **Timestamps aren't ordered along this pair** — only 52.55% of matched pairs have `purchase_completed.timestamp ≥ pay_now_clicked.timestamp`. This is the same non-monotonic-timestamp trap documented as [D1](../../../context/known_issues.md) for `document_uploaded → purchase_completed` (52.2%), now confirmed to extend to `pay_now_clicked → purchase_completed` too.
2. Even restricted to the "monotonic" 3,707 pairs, the naive average diff is **4,614,076 ms (~76.9 minutes)**, with a median of ~69 minutes — clearly not a payment-step latency at all, but the whole application-session gap (consistent with `relationship.md`'s separately-verified "110.5 minutes app-start-to-purchase" figure). `pay_now_clicked`/`purchase_completed` timestamps simply aren't tight enough around the actual payment action to serve as a `shown → confirmed` proxy.

On top of that, `express_payment_confirmed`'s 836 users have **0% overlap** with `purchase_completed`'s 7,054 users — consistent with [D2](../../../context/known_issues.md)'s finding that all 5 Express Checkout tables are a standalone population relative to the main funnel — so even a valid standard-side latency couldn't be paired user-for-user with Express; only population-level comparison would ever be possible, and that channel is broken by point 1–2 above.

**Bottom line:** Express's own payment step takes ~2.3 seconds on average (836 confirmed payments). Whether that is "faster" than the standard flow cannot be honestly quantified from this database — the standard flow has no comparable instrumented latency metric, and the nearest available proxy (timestamp subtraction) is invalidated by the same timestamp non-monotonicity documented in known_issues.md D1.
