## Question
Does Express lift checkout → success conversion vs standard checkout, and by how much?

## Answer
Yes — Express checkout converts far higher: **83.02%** (836/1,007 users, set-membership
verified) from `express_checkout_selected` → `express_payment_confirmed`, vs **47.86%**
(7,054/14,739 users, set-membership verified) from `pay_now_clicked` → `purchase_completed`
for standard checkout over H1 2026. That's a **+35.2 percentage-point** lift, or **1.73×
(+73% relative)**. Re-running the standard-checkout rate restricted to the express sample's
exact date window (2026-06-08→2026-06-28: 858/1,792 = 47.88%) gives essentially the same
baseline, so the gap isn't a time-window artifact.

**Scope and caveats:**
- Standard checkout: `pay_now_clicked`→`purchase_completed`, all of H1 2026 (2026-01-01→2026-07-01), n=14,739. Express checkout: `express_checkout_selected`→`express_payment_confirmed`, sample window 2026-06-08→2026-06-28, n=1,007 — role labels per [tables/index.md](../../../context/tables/index.md) ("checkout" and "express checkout"/"express conversion" respectively).
- Both rates were computed by `uniqExact` **set membership** on `user_id` (per known_issues.md D1), not `windowFunnel`/row-count ratios — the express result (836/1,007) matches the row-count ratio already in the wiki, confirming the express flow is perfectly nested by `user_id`.
- This is **not a randomized or matched comparison**. `application_id` does not join Express Checkout's 5 tables to the main funnel (known_issues.md D2, 0% overlap, re-confirmed table-by-table), so the 1,007 Express converters and the 14,739 standard-checkout population cannot be verified as comparable cohorts (device, geo, funnel stage reached, etc.) — only their aggregate rates can be compared.
- **Likely selection bias**: Express is opt-in — only 61.0% of users shown the button (1,007/1,650) selected it. Users who choose a saved-method, one-tap flow may already skew toward higher purchase intent than the general `pay_now_clicked` population, so some of the 35pp gap is plausibly self-selection rather than a pure product/UX effect. This dataset cannot separate the two.
- Sample size: Express n=1,007 vs standard n=14,739 — directionally solid but a much smaller, single 3-week sample vs a 6-month baseline.
- Both flows share a similar unexplained-leak pattern: Express has a ~101-row gap between `otp_success=true` (937) and `express_payment_confirmed` (836) that no column explains, mirroring the baseline's own unexplained `pay_now_clicked→purchase_completed` leak (~52% never convert) — noted for context, it doesn't change the headline comparison.
